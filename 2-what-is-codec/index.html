What is a Codec? Complete Guide to H.264, H.265, ProRes & RAW

Why does your editing timeline stutter and lag? Why do exports take hours, and which file formats are actually optimal for post-production? Practically all these performance challenges come down to your video codec. Here is complete professional clarity.

By Ajay K MeenaUpdated: August 202610 min read

Whenever a video editor turns to me and complains, "My DaVinci Resolve is constantly lagging and freezing during playback," nine times out of ten the real underlying issue is their choice of codec, not a defective hardware graphics card. Before upgrading expensive desktop components, every filmmaker must master the technical fundamentals of how video codecs function and how they differ from simple storage containers.

Codec vs Container — The Most Important Distinction

Codec (short for COder-DECoder) is the mathematical compression algorithm that encodes video stream data during camera recording, and subsequently decodes that data during timeline playback and exporting. A Container (represented by file extensions such as .mp4, .mov, or .mxf) is merely a structural digital packaging wrapper that groups together the encoded video track, accompanying audio channels, timecode, and camera camera metadata.

This is precisely why a single .mov file might contain heavily compressed consumer H.264 video, or uncompressed professional Apple ProRes 4444 video. Both files utilize an identical outer MOV packaging container, but possess entirely different internal compression architectures. Never attempt to guess an editing clip's playback complexity by reading its file extension; always check the specific internal codec attributes using MediaInfo or Resolve's clip properties panel.

How Video Compression Operates: I-Frames, P-Frames, and B-Frames

Modern video compression algorithms manage massive digital storage footprints by classifying video motion into three specialized frame architecture types:

  • I-Frame (Intra-Frame / Keyframe): A self-contained, independent visual image. It encodes the entire picture across every single pixel without requiring algorithmic referencing from neighboring timeline frames.
  • P-Frame (Predictive Frame): A highly abbreviated visual instruction frame that stores only specific physical movement changes occurring since the previous sequential reference frame.
  • B-Frame (Bi-Directional Predictive Frame): An analytical transition frame that cross-references both preceding and forthcoming surrounding frames to calculate differential pixel shifts.

When you attempt to playback or fast-forward to a compressed P-Frame or B-Frame on your editing timeline, your computer's CPU cannot simply project that image directly onto the monitor screen. Instead, DaVinci Resolve must dynamically locate the nearest baseline I-Frame, calculate every sequential algebraic mathematical variation across subsequent intermediate frames, and reconstruct the final viewable image in real-time. This demanding computational overhead causes standard camera-native H.264 and H.265 footage to playback with frustrating stuttering and lag on editing timelines.

The 3 Primary Categories of Video Codecs

Long-GOP Delivery Codecs
LOSSY · INTERFRAME COMPRESSION

Common Examples: H.264 / AVC, H.265 / HEVC, XAVC-S, AVCHD

These highly compressed formats are designed primarily for in-camera SD memory card acquisition and final internet distribution. They achieve compact storage footprints and remarkable visual fidelity per megabyte by discarding redundant spatial information across sequential frame groupings (Group of Pictures). However, they impose substantial decoding strain upon computer processors during non-linear timeline editing.

Advantages

  • Extremely efficient, compact file storage size
  • Ideal format for streaming, web upload, and client playback
  • Universally supported across mobile devices and internet web browsers

Disadvantages

  • Demands intensive processor decoding, causing noticeable timeline editing lag
  • Subject to compounding visual generation loss across repetitive transcode exports
  • Prone to visible color banding during intense DaVinci Resolve secondary color grading (8-bit variants)
Intra-Frame Editing Codecs
MEZZANINE · INSTANT TIMELINE SCRUBBING

Common Examples: Apple ProRes (Proxy / LT / 422 / 422 HQ / 4444), Avid DNxHR / DNxHD, CineForm

With intra-frame encoding architecture, every single frame across your timeline is captured as an autonomous, uncompressed independent photograph (100% I-Frames). Because the computer editing processor does not waste clock cycles calculating predictive motion equations between adjacent frames, timeline playback and dynamic cursor scrubbing respond instantaneously without frame drops.

Advantages

  • Silky-smooth, instantaneous timeline scrubbing and multi-cam playback
  • High mathematical color accuracy with robust multi-generation visual endurance
  • Widely available in professional high bit-depth color variations (10-bit to 12-bit)

Disadvantages

  • Consumes approximately five to ten times more disk space than equivalent H.264 files
  • Requires high-speed external NVMe SSD storage hardware and dedicated initial ingestion transcode time
  • Direct internal recording is generally limited to professional cinema cameras and dedicated field monitors
Uncompressed & Compressed RAW Formats
PURE SENSOR DATA

Common Examples: Blackmagic RAW (BRAW), ARRIRAW, RED R3D, CinemaDNG

A RAW video recording is not a standardized color imaging file; rather, it represents raw, unbaked photosite electrical voltage readings extracted directly from your camera's image sensor. Fundamental photographic parameters including ISO value, Kelvin color temperature white balance, and tint are preserved as flexible mathematical metadata variables. This grants colorists boundless artistic freedom during grading sessions, albeit demanding substantial computer workstation processing architecture.

Advantages

  • Enables effortless post-production adjustment of exposure ISO and white balance without destructive artifacts
  • Preserves the absolute widest theoretical dynamic range ceiling from the camera sensor
  • Provides the ultimate professional color latitude for cinematic grade styling

Disadvantages

  • Generates substantial data file footprints requiring high-capacity NVMe media hardware
  • Relies heavily on graphics card GPU power to calculate real-time color debayering formulas
  • Restricted primarily to professional cinema production cameras and specialized recording monitors

Professional Editing Workflow: The Proxy Strategy

When working with standard camera-native H.264 or H.265 video footage, never force your primary video timeline to edit those heavy files directly—you will invariably encounter sluggish playback and dropped frames. Instead, utilize DaVinci Resolve's integrated Generate Optimized Media or Proxy Media Generator tools to create lightweight, editable intra-frame duplicates (such as ProRes Proxy or DNxHR LB). You can cut your sequence seamlessly using these responsive proxies; when you finally initialize your master Deliver page rendering job, DaVinci automatically reconnects to your pristine native high-resolution camera clips for maximum fidelity export.

Pro tip: Always store and execute your working editing proxies directly from a high-speed solid-state NVMe or SATA SSD drive rather than a mechanical desktop spinning disk HDD. Traditional internal hard drives cannot execute random data seek operations rapidly enough to supply multiple concurrent video preview streams without inducing data read bottleneck delays.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the precise difference between a video codec and a file container?

A container (represented by extensions like MP4 or MOV) acts as a standardized digital packaging envelope that stores video tracks, audio channels, and camera metadata together in one file. A codec is the underlying structural encoding algorithm responsible for compressing and decompressing the actual moving image stream inside that container envelope.

Should I edit my video projects natively using H.264 camera files?

While modern computer processors offer basic hardware acceleration for H.264 files, professional editors avoid editing directly with Long-GOP interframe codecs whenever possible due to timeline scrubbing delays. Establishing a standardized ProRes or DNxHR proxy transcode workflow ensures smooth, error-free editing timelines.

When is shooting in a RAW video format genuinely necessary?

Shooting in uncompressed or compressed RAW video formats is recommended for commercial ad campaigns, cinematic narrative films, or complex lighting environments where precise post-production recovery of extreme highlight and shadow detail is vital. For routine YouTube vlogs, social media reels, or fast-turnaround interviews, high-bitrate 10-bit Log recording offers an excellent quality balance without consuming excessive disk capacity.
