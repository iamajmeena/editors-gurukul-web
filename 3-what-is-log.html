What is Log? S-Log, C-Log, V-Log, F-Log, N-Log Explained

Log footage washed-out kyun dikhta hai, aur har camera brand ka apna Log kyun hai — dynamic range, gamma curve aur grading approach ka poora breakdown.

By Ajay K MeenaUpdated: July 202611 min read

Jab pehli baar Log footage dekhte ho, lagta hai camera kharaab ho gayi — flat, grey, low contrast. Lekin yeahi flatness hai jo asli power hai. Samjho kaise.

Log Kaam Kaise Karta Hai

Camera sensor light ko linear tareeke se capture karta hai — lekin human eye aur standard video (Rec.709) non-linear/logarithmic tareeke se light perceive karte hain. Agar sensor ka linear data seedha Rec.709 gamma mein map kar diya jaaye, toh highlights turant clip ho jaate hain aur shadows crush ho jaate hain — dynamic range waste ho jaata hai.

Log encoding is linear data ko ek logarithmic curve pe redistribute karta hai — jyada "bits" shadows aur midtones ko diye jaate hain jahan human eye zyada sensitive hai, aur highlights compress ho jaati hain bina clip kiye. Result: ek flat, desaturated image jisme sensor ka poora dynamic range preserve hota hai, ready for grading.

Brand-by-Brand Breakdown

Sony — S-Log2 / S-Log3

S-Log2 purana hai, 8-bit cameras ke liye better suited (A7S II jaisi bodies). S-Log3 newer aur industry standard hai — Cineon log curve pe based, toe se linear hai, grading mein zyada predictable. S-Gamut3.Cine color space ke saath pair hota hai for cinema-accurate colors.

Canon — C-Log / C-Log2 / C-Log3

C-Log2 sabse zyada dynamic range deta hai (~16 stops) but grading mein zyada steps chahiye hote hain. C-Log3 easier-to-grade version hai, thoda kam range ke saath but faster workflow. Cinema EOS line (C70, C300) mein full family milta hai, R-series mein limited.

Panasonic — V-Log / V-Log L

V-Log full version cinema line (S1H, BS1) mein milta hai. V-Log L GH-series ke liye lighter version hai — kai models mein paid firmware upgrade ke through unlock hota hai. ARRI LUTs ke saath achhi tarah compatible hai kyunki curve ARRI Log C se inspired hai.

Benefits of Shooting in Log

  • Maximum dynamic range capture — highlights aur shadows dono preserve hote hain
  • Grading flexibility — ek hi footage ko multiple looks mein grade kar sakte ho
  • Consistent base for LUTs — cinematic LUTs Log footage pe predictably kaam karte hain
  • Multi-camera matching — different cameras ka footage Log mein aasani se match hota hai

Limitations — Jo Log Ki "Advanced Guide" Mein Koi Nahi Batata

  • Exposure critical hai: Log ko ETTR (Expose To The Right) approach chahiye — underexpose karne se shadow noise badh jaata hai
  • 8-bit + Log = banding risk: Agar camera sirf 8-bit record karta hai, heavy grading mein color banding aayega
  • Monitoring difficult hai: Flat image dekh kar exposure judge karna mushkil hai — LUT preview ya false color zaroori hai
  • Grading skill chahiye: Log footage bina grade kiye unusable hai — yeh RAW jaisa hi commitment hai
  • Storage aur processing overhead: Log footage aksar higher bitrate mein record hota hai
Golden rule: Agar aapke paas 10-bit 4:2:2 recording capability nahi hai, toh full Log se better hoga camera ka flat/Cine-style picture profile use karna — kam dynamic range milega but banding ka risk bhi kam hoga.

FAQs

Log footage flat aur washed out kyun dikhta hai?

Kyunki Log profile sensor ke pure dynamic range ko ek logarithmic curve mein compress karta hai bina contrast ya saturation baked-in kiye, taaki grading mein maximum flexibility mile.

Kya beginners ko Log mein shoot karna chahiye?

Agar aap grading seekh rahe ho toh haan, but agar time nahi hai grading ke liye, toh camera ke flat ya standard profile mein shoot karna safer hai.

8-bit camera mein Log shoot karna sahi hai?

Kar sakte ho but banding ka risk zyada hota hai heavy grading mein. Exposure sahi rakhna (ETTR) aur halki grading approach 8-bit Log ke saath best practice hai.
