Why Do Cheap Wireless Microphones Sound Like "Robotic Underwater Audio"?

When consumers purchase entry-level wireless microphone kits priced near the ₹2,000 threshold, the most widespread user complaint heard across editing studios is universal: "The instant I press the Active Noise Cancellation (ANC) button, my dialogue immediately transforms into thin, artificial, underwater-style robotic distortion."

This occurs because bargain-tier hardware relies on aggressive, underpowered internal Digital Signal Processing (DSP) computer microchips. Instead of analyzing and separating complex vocal formants from static environmental background noise, these primitive algorithm chips aggressively shear off entire audio frequency bands. Along with slicing out low-frequency ceiling fan hum and air conditioning airflow, they simultaneously eradicate the lower mid-range frequency harmonics that give human speech natural depth and authority. When recording inside quiet interior studios or untreated home rooms, keep hardware Noise Cancellation completely OFF and manually deactivate noisy household ceiling fans. Engage wireless digital ANC strictly when recording outdoors amid inescapable, loud civic traffic or intense structural wind noise.
