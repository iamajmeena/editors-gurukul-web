The 180-Degree Shutter Rule Explained

The 180-degree shutter rule is the cinema standard for exposure timing. It dictates that your camera shutter speed should always be set to 1 ÷ (2 × Frame Rate). This reproduces the natural motion blur that human eyes perceive when objects move through space.

Quick FAQ

Q: What happens if I shoot at 24fps with a 1/500s shutter speed?

Your video will lose natural motion blur. Moving subjects will look jittery, strobed, and robotic (often called the 'Saving Private Ryan' action look).

Q: Why do Cinema Cameras use Shutter Angle instead of Shutter Speed?

When you shoot with a Cinema Camera (like BMPCC 6K or RED) in Shutter Angle mode (180°), the camera automatically adjusts the shutter speed whenever you change frame rates from 24fps to 60fps, keeping motion blur perfectly proportional without manual recalculation.
