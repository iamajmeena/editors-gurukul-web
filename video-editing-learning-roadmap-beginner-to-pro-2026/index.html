Video Editing Learning Roadmap: Beginner to Pro in 6 Months (2026)

When I started editing, I watched random YouTube tutorials for months and felt like I was going nowhere. The problem wasn't lack of content — there are thousands of tutorials. The problem was no structure. I didn't know what to learn first, what to skip, and when to move on. This roadmap is the structured path I wish I had when I started.

What this is: A month-by-month learning path that takes you from "I've never opened an editing app" to "I can deliver professional work and charge for it." Follow it in order. Don't skip months. Each stage builds on the previous one.

Month 1: Software Fundamentals

Goal: Be comfortable navigating your editor and making basic cuts without panic.

Week 1-2: Interface & Navigation

  • Install DaVinci Resolve Free (see our software comparison if unsure)
  • Learn the workspace: Media Pool, Source Viewer, Timeline, Program Monitor
  • Import footage, create a project, set timeline resolution and frame rate
  • Practice: Import 10 random clips from your phone. Drop them on a timeline. Play them back.

Week 3-4: Basic Editing Operations

  • Cut (C), Trim, Ripple Delete — the 3 operations you'll use 80% of the time
  • In/Out points (I and O keys) — mark sections before dragging to timeline
  • Adding transitions (cross dissolve — the only transition professionals use regularly)
  • Adding text/titles on screen
  • Exporting your first video (H.264, 1080p) — see our export settings guide
  • Practice project: Edit a 2-minute vlog from your phone footage. Export it. Watch it. Note what feels wrong.

Learn These Keyboard Shortcuts Now:

J-K-L (playback), I-O (in/out), C (cut), V (selection), Space (play/pause), Ctrl+Z (undo). These 8 shortcuts will make you 3x faster immediately. Full list in our shortcuts cheat sheet.

Month 2: Editing Rhythm & Pacing

Goal: Understand WHY you cut, not just HOW. Develop a feel for rhythm.

Core Concepts:

  • Cut on action — cut when someone moves, gestures, or turns. The motion hides the edit.
  • Cut on dialogue — cut when the speaker changes or pauses naturally.
  • Pacing — fast cuts = energy, excitement. Slow cuts = calm, emotional, dramatic. Match the pacing to the content's mood.
  • The invisible edit — the best edits are ones the viewer doesn't notice. If your audience is thinking about your editing, you've failed.

Exercises:

  1. Re-edit a YouTube video. Find a popular creator's video, download it, and re-edit with better pacing. Compare yours to theirs. Read our pacing & rhythm guide.
  2. Music video exercise. Take 20-30 random clips (B-roll, stock footage, your own). Cut them to a song. Every cut should land on a beat or musical transition. This trains your rhythmic instinct.
  3. Watch films actively. Pick any film you love. Watch 10 minutes and count every cut. Note: when does the editor cut? Why? What would happen if the cut was 1 second later?

Month 3: Audio & Sound Design

Goal: Understand that audio is 50% of the viewing experience and learn to mix properly.

What to Learn:

  • Audio levels: Dialogue at -12 to -6 dBFS, music at -25 to -18 dBFS
  • Audio ducking — automatically lower music when dialogue plays
  • Basic EQ — remove low-frequency rumble (high-pass filter at 80-100 Hz)
  • Sound effects — whooshes, clicks, impacts. Where to find them (see our music & SFX guide)
  • Room tone — recording 30 seconds of "silence" in your recording environment to fill gaps

Read our Fairlight Audio Post guide for the complete audio workflow in DaVinci Resolve.

Practice Project:

Take any 2-minute video with bad audio. Clean it up: remove background noise, normalize levels, add music and 3 sound effects. Export and compare before/after. You'll be shocked at the difference.

Month 4: Color Correction & Grading Basics

Goal: Make your footage look consistent, clean, and intentional.

Color Correction (Technical — Getting It Right):

  • White balance correction using the eyedropper tool
  • Exposure normalization using Lift/Gamma/Gain
  • Using scopes: Waveform (exposure), Vectorscope (color balance), Parade (RGB channels)
  • Shot matching — making different clips from different takes/cameras look consistent

Color Grading (Creative — Making It Beautiful):

  • Understanding the node tree structure in Resolve
  • Adding contrast with S-curves
  • Creating mood with color temperature shifts
  • Skin tone isolation and protection

Essential reading: Our Nodes Guide, White Balance Method, and LOG Grading Step-by-Step.

Month 6: Portfolio, Pricing & First Client

Goal: Turn your skills into income. Get your first paying client.

Action Steps:

  1. Build your portfolio: 5-7 best pieces. Include at least one spec edit (re-edited creator video). Read our Portfolio Building Guide.
  2. Set your initial rates: Start at market rate for beginners (₹1,500-5,000 per YouTube video, ₹500-2,000 per Reel). See our Editor Rates India guide.
  3. Send 20 cold DMs to small creators on Instagram. Offer a free sample edit. Convert interest into paid projects.
  4. Create profiles on Upwork and Fiverr. Your first 3-5 projects will be low-paying. That's fine — you're building reviews.
  5. Deliver on time, communicate clearly, accept feedback gracefully. Professionalism gets referrals. Referrals build careers.
The 6-month truth: After 6 months of dedicated daily practice (1-2 hours/day), you'll be competent enough to handle entry-level paid work. You won't be "pro" — that takes 2-3 years. But you'll be good enough to earn while you learn, which is the best learning path there is. The worst thing you can do is spend years "learning" without shipping real work.

After Month 6: The Ongoing Journey

  • Edit something every week. Skills decay without practice.
  • Study films and videos you admire. Break down why they work. Steal techniques (not footage).
  • Upgrade your hardware when your earnings justify it. See our laptop guide.
  • Learn client management. Contracts, revision limits, scope creep — the business skills nobody teaches but everyone needs.
  • Stay current. Follow this site, join r/VideoEditing and r/colorgrading on Reddit, and watch what professional editors share on YouTube and Instagram.

FAQ

Can I learn video editing in 1 month?

You can learn the basic software operations in 1 month. But editing is a craft — understanding pacing, storytelling, audio, and color takes time. After 1 month, you can make basic cuts. After 3 months, you can make decent YouTube videos. After 6 months, you can take on paid work. After 2 years, you're genuinely good.

Do I need to learn After Effects too?

Not immediately. DaVinci Resolve's Fusion page handles most motion graphics needs. Learn After Effects in Year 2 if you want to specialize in motion graphics or work at agencies where Adobe is the standard. For most freelance editing work, Resolve + basic Fusion is enough.

Is it worth getting a certification?

The Blackmagic Design DaVinci Resolve certification is the only one I'd recommend — it's affordable, respected by studios, and the training material is excellent. Adobe certifications are less valued in the freelance market. But remember: no client has ever asked for my certificate. They ask for my portfolio.

1 hour a day or 7 hours on weekends — which is better?

1 hour daily. Consistency builds muscle memory. After a 7-day gap, you'll spend the first hour just remembering where things are. Daily practice, even 30 minutes, beats weekend marathons for skill building.
